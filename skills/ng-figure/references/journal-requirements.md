# Verified sources and boundaries

Checked 2026-10-16. Recheck for new manuscripts or final production. Prefer specific editor instructions and journal-specific guidance.

## Nature Genetics

https://www.nature.com/ng/submission-guidelines/aip-and-formatting

- Maximum width: 180 mm; raster panels: at least 300 dpi at final size.
- Standard labels: sans serif, 5–7 pt; Symbol for Greek characters.
- Keep image labels, scale bars and error bars editable; use scale bars.
- Legends describe estimates, uncertainty, sample sizes, tests and P values where applicable.
- Extended Data figures fit one PDF page. Supplementary Figures fit one PDF page together with their legends.

The final-manuscript PDF restriction does not prohibit PDF figure artwork. The page gives no numeric single-column width or maximum figure height. Distinguish Extended Data from Supplementary Figures.

## General Nature advice, not journal-specific NG rules

https://research-figure-guide.nature.com/figures/preparing-figures-our-specifications/

Use standard sans-serif type, editable vector artwork, embedded fonts, RGB and accessible colors. Avoid outlined text, Type 3 fonts, crowded labels and decorative clutter. General panel tags are bold upright lowercase at 8 pt. Matplotlib PDF fonttype 42 is recommended. The page gives 300 dpi in image advice and 450 dpi in its export section; 600 dpi for selective rasterization is a conservative house default, not an NG requirement.

https://research-figure-guide.nature.com/figures/building-and-exporting-figure-panels/

This guide targets flagship Nature. Do not present its 89/183 mm widths or 170 mm height ceiling as verified NG requirements. Use the NG 180 mm width maximum; our 90 mm single-column working width and content-dependent height are choices.

## Shelley’s preferences

Use elegant restrained colors, high legibility, consistent semantics, editable PDFs for Illustrator/macOS, reproducible scripts and final visual inspection. These do not guarantee editorial acceptance. Do not force one fixed palette on all scientific variables.
